# Code of Conduct

This project asks four things of everyone who takes part, whatever else they may disagree about, so that collaboration
stays productive, constructive and fair. Be civil, act in good faith, be decent and be productive.

## Be civil

Be courteous. Bullying, harassment, personal attacks and publishing anyone's private information are out of bounds. None
of it is productive, and none of it is decent. Criticise the work as hard as the work deserves. Never the person.

## Act in good faith

Assume that others are acting in good faith, and show it yourself by contributing to improve the work. Constructive
criticism is welcome and expected. Constructive means about the work and offered in good faith. It does not need to be
gentle, and it does not require you to bring a solution. Saying plainly that something is wrong or flawed, and why, is
constructive. Disagreement about the work is normal, and raising a risk or a failure case counts as a contribution, not
a complaint. Do not present your own view, your fork or your employer's position as the project's.

## Be decent

Treat people with respect, whoever they are and whatever they believe. Respect here means civility towards the person,
not agreement with their beliefs. People who hold very different views are equally welcome to contribute and support the
project's success. This project takes no position on anyone's beliefs, and it does not ask anyone to hide theirs.

## Be productive

Focus on the work. You may bring your own views, beliefs and opinions into a discussion when they are relevant to the
work and you can argue their merit, whatever they are about. The project decides questions on their merits. A decision
settles what this project will do, not whose values are right. A discussion that has drifted from the work, or has lost
its merit, will be closed or cut back to the part that is still about the work, regardless of who started it. That is
not a judgement on anyone.

Contribute work you have checked yourself and can explain, whether it was written by hand or generated by a tool.
Submissions left for the maintainer to sort out are not productive. The standard is care, not polish. Unsolicited bulk
posting, flooding, repeated pinging and using project spaces to recruit or advertise are out of bounds, and that content
is removed without discussion.

## Decisions

This project is part of the Astrana project, and any policy Astrana sets for its projects applies here. Both have the
same maintainer, who has final say on every decision, technical or otherwise, and who holds to this code like everyone
else. Decisions are final, and the maintainer explains them to the people they affect. If you disagree with one, set out
your reasons once, on the merits, and answer any questions asked about them. When the maintainer has answered, the
matter is settled. The maintainer reopens it only if something material has changed, and decides whether it has.

## Where this applies

This code applies in the project's spaces (issues, pull requests, discussions and the project's own communication
channels), to what is contributed to the repository (code, comments, commit messages and documentation), and whenever
someone is speaking for the project elsewhere.

It does not govern what anyone believes. Beyond the spaces above, conduct outside the project is not this project's
concern. The one exception is bullying, harassment or publishing private information directed at a participant because
of their participation here. That conduct reaches into the project's spaces by driving people out of them, so it is this
project's concern wherever it happens. Criticism of the project or of anyone's work, wherever it appears, is not
bullying or harassment.

Personal issues between contributors are not this project's concern, and the project will not take sides in them or try
to resolve them. Bullying, harassment and publishing private information are never treated as a personal issue. If a
personal issue materially affects the project's ability to stay productive, the project will deal with the disruption to
the work, not the dispute.

## Raising a problem

Most disagreements between people come from misunderstandings, and talking to the person directly and civilly usually
resolves them. Do that first when it is reasonable.

If that does not resolve it, or if someone has breached this code, write to conduct@astrana.org rather than arguing it
out in public. Talking to the person first is encouraged, not required. A report made in good faith carries no penalty,
whatever the outcome. A report that is knowingly false, or made to harass, is itself a breach of this code. Security
vulnerabilities are a different matter. Report them privately, as described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

The maintainer reads every report, replies to it and handles it in private. The same applies to a report about the
maintainer, because there is no one else to read it. The maintainer may also act on conduct seen directly, without
waiting for a report. The code itself and the public record of how it has been applied hold the maintainer to it.

Before deciding anything about a person, the maintainer tells them what the complaint is and asks for their side. The
only exception is an account that exists solely to spam or advertise. The maintainer may still lock a discussion or hide
content in the meantime. Your identity is not shared without your agreement. If the complaint cannot be put to the
person without identifying you, you will be asked whether you are willing to be named. If you are not, the maintainer
will not decide against the person on the report alone.

Responses are proportionate to the conduct and to any history of it, which can mean anything from a private message to
removal of content or of access to the project. When access is withdrawn, or content is removed because a participant
has breached this code, a short public note records which part of this code applied and what was done. It does not name
the reporter, and it is not a place for discussion. Warnings stay private, and closing or cutting back a discussion, or
removing spam, needs no note. Removing a person's access does not remove their work. Accepted contributions are not
taken out because of a conduct decision, and they remain under the project's licence with authorship intact, unless the
law or the licence requires otherwise.

## Changes to this code

The maintainer may revise this code. Its history is in the repository.
