# 1. Each instance has one owner, who is its sole administrator

Proposed.

## Context

Astrana treats the individual as the basic unit of society. Families, communities and every other group are made up of
individuals, and arise from the voluntary connections between them rather than from a structure above them. An instance
is one installation of Astrana belonging to one person. Someone has to hold authority over the instance, its settings
and every account on it, and where that authority sits decides who answers for the instance and who could act against
the person using it.

## Decision

Each instance has one owner, who is its only administrator. There is no operator and no authority of any kind above the
owner. Other people can use an instance too, but authority over it never leaves the owner.

## Consequences

- Nobody can suspend an owner or their instance, because there is no company above them.
- An owner is the operator of a service they run. A takedown notice, a rights claim or a police enquiry reaches them
  directly, because there is nobody in between.
- Nobody is coming to help if something breaks.
- Where a child owns an instance, a parent or guardian's authority over its parental controls is the one bounded
  exception, and it covers nothing else.
- Shared or delegated administration, such as a household or a couple running one instance together, was rejected,
  because authority over the instance would then leave the person it represents.
