# Mission and vision

Every [decision record](adr/README.md) rests on the mission and vision below. A record that cannot be traced back to
them needs a reason of its own.

## Mission

To build social networking software that is materially healthier than what people have now, by shaping the technology to
the whole of human nature rather than to whichever parts of it are commercially profitable, and to the interests of the
people using it rather than anybody else's.

## Vision

A place of your own, for the people already well known to you, where relationships are tended and deepened over a
lifetime. Yours to own, never competing for your attention, seen only by the people you choose, and keeping only what
matters.

## The premise

The individual is the basic unit of society. Astrana is free and open source, and is built so that each person has an
instance of their own. Instances connect directly to each other, with no third party required in between. A child can
have an instance too, and a parent or guardian holds authority over that instance's parental controls.

## The commitments

### For the people already well known to you

There is no discovery, no directory and no strangers. You connect with someone only when you both agree.

The cost is that people can ask to connect with you only if they have your address, and Astrana never suggests anyone to
connect with.

### Yours to own

Your instance runs on hardware or hosting you choose, and no platform sits above you that could suspend you. Nothing
about your instance is changed without you, and you can take everything with you if you leave.

The cost is that you are responsible for your instance, and look after it yourself or have someone you trust do it for
you.

### Never competing for your attention

Nothing is ranked to hold your attention, and nothing invents a reason to come back, such as a streak or a reminder. The
feed stops when you reach the end, rather than scrolling forever.

The cost is that you can miss what someone shares unless you go back and look for it, because Astrana will not remind
you about it.

### Seen only by the people you choose

When you share something, you choose who can see it, and Astrana shows it only to them and to anyone responsible for
them. Apart from the least a connection needs in order to work, nothing about you leaves your instance unless you choose
to send it, and Astrana keeps no more about anyone than it needs.

The cost is a small choice each time you share.

### Keeping only what matters

What you share disappears after a set time unless you deliberately choose to keep it.

The cost is that anything not kept is deleted from your instance when its time is up.

### Respectful of where and how you live

Astrana helps you follow the laws you are subject to, but never enforces them on you. You set the rules of your own
place, so no single culture's customs or traditions are imposed on anyone.

The cost is that Astrana cannot make sure an instance meets every local law, because you, not Astrana, are answerable
for your own place and jurisdiction.

## When things conflict

When two principles or goals pull in different directions, the choice that is better for the wellbeing of the people
using Astrana wins. When a technically better design works against the mission, the mission wins, even if the result is
slower, plainer or harder to build.

Some things are never traded away:

- Success is never measured by how much people use Astrana.
- Nothing in Astrana makes money from the time people spend using it or the attention they give it.
- No adult's choices are taken away to protect them from every risk.
- Astrana never claims to protect people more than it does.
- No single loss locks you out of your instance for good.
- Security is never weakened for convenience. Where Astrana can do without something, it leaves it out rather than
  switching it off, because what is not there cannot be attacked.
- Anything that is not needed for people to share with and stay connected to those they know stays off until the
  instance's owner turns it on, or a guardian does for a child.
